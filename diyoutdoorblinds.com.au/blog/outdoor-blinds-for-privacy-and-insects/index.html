Which outdoor blinds give privacy and keep mosquitoes out?

Privacy and insect protection are two different jobs. One is about what people can see through the fabric; the other is about what can get around it.

Privacy · 7 min read · Updated October 2026

Short answer: For privacy, choose a mesh with a low openness factor in a dark colour, and remember any mesh becomes see-through at night when your side is lit. For insects, use a fine mesh or PVC and, more importantly, a track-guided blind with sealed sides and a bottom bar that sits on the floor. Gaps let insects in, not the fabric.

A lot of people buy outdoor blinds for a view they don't want — a neighbour's window, a side street — or for evenings ruined by mosquitoes. Both are solvable, but the fabric choice only does half the work.

How private is outdoor blind mesh?

Mesh is described by its openness factor: the percentage of the fabric that is open weave. A 5% mesh is 95% solid; a 10% mesh lets more light and view through.

Mesh openness and privacy (general guide)
OpennessDaytime privacyView outAirflow
1–3%HighReducedLower
5%GoodGoodModerate
10%ModerateVery goodHigher

Colour matters as much as openness. Dark mesh — charcoal, black — gives the clearest view out, while lighter colours reflect more light, which reduces how much people outside can see in during the day but also softens your view out.

Why can neighbours see in at night?

Mesh privacy depends on light. During the day the outside is brighter than your covered area, so people outside see the fabric surface rather than through it. At night, with lights on under the verandah, that reverses: you are the bright side, and anyone outside can see in through mesh.

If night-time privacy matters, you have three options:

  • Blockout or very low-openness fabric on the side facing the neighbours
  • A second blind on that opening, mesh for day and blockout for night
  • Lighting placement — lights aimed down and away from the blind reduce how lit-up the fabric is

Clear PVC gives no privacy at all, day or night. Tinted PVC gives a little during the day.

Do outdoor blinds keep mosquitoes out?

They can, but the gaps decide it. Mosquitoes, midges and flies do not need to get through the fabric if they can go around it.

Choose a fabric with a fine weave

Low-openness mesh and solid PVC stop most flying insects. Very small midges can pass through more open weaves, so if those are your problem, go finer.

Seal the sides

This is where track-guided blinds earn their keep. The fabric edge is held in a track down each side, leaving no gap. Rope-and-pulley and straight-drop blinds have open edges that insects get straight past.

Seal the bottom

Add 50 mm to a face-fit drop so the bottom bar sits down on the floor, and check the floor is level across the opening. A brush or rubber seal on the bottom bar helps on decking with gaps between boards.

Seal the top

A face-fit headbox that overlaps the beam closes the strip above the blind. If your roof leaves an open gap above the beam, insects will find it — a pelmet or infill strip closes it.

Don't forget every side

An enclosure is only as insect-proof as its weakest side. One open side, a gap at a corner post or a door left ajar undoes the rest.

What about gaps around doors and windows?

If your outdoor area connects to the house through sliding doors or windows, those openings are part of the insect story too. A sealed blind enclosure keeps insects out of the outdoor room, but any that get in will follow you inside when the door opens. Fly screens on the house doors and windows close that second line.

Also check for small gaps you may not notice in daylight: the space where a track meets an uneven post, an unsealed downpipe penetration, or the corner where a side blind meets a house wall. Walking the enclosure at dusk with a torch from inside shows up light leaks, and anywhere light gets through, insects can too.

What about pets and children?

Loose cords and chains on blinds are a strangulation risk for young children. In Australia, corded internal window coverings are subject to mandatory safety standards, and the same caution makes sense outdoors. Practical steps:

  • Choose cordless systems where children play — track-guided blinds with a push-up bottom bar have no cords at all
  • Where cords exist, keep them tensioned and secured high, out of reach
  • Keep furniture that children can climb away from cords

For pets, the bottom bar is the weak point. A dog that pushes under a blind will eventually stretch the fabric or pop the edge out of the track. A bottom-bar lock keeps the blind down and the pet in.

Do outdoor blinds keep flies away when you're eating outside?

They help a great deal, but only when the space is closed. Flies come in whenever a blind is raised or a door is left open, and once inside they tend to stay. Lowering the blinds before you bring food out, and keeping one opening as the door, works better than raising and lowering several blinds through a meal. If you want a door-sized opening that stays insect-proof, a zip-down panel or separate screen door on that opening is worth considering.

Can a blind give privacy without blocking the breeze?

Partly. Privacy and airflow pull in opposite directions: the more a fabric blocks the view, the more it blocks air. A 5% mesh is a common compromise, giving good daytime privacy while still letting a breeze through. On the side facing a neighbour, use the lowest openness you are comfortable with; on the sides facing your own garden, a more open mesh keeps the space feeling ventilated.

Putting it together

For a typical alfresco area overlooked on one side, a common setup is a 5% charcoal mesh track-guided blind on the open sides for daytime shade and insect protection, and a lower-openness or blockout fabric on the side facing the neighbours.

Common questions

Can you see through outdoor mesh blinds at night?

Yes, when your side is lit. At night the covered area becomes brighter than outside, so people can see in through mesh. Use blockout fabric where night privacy matters.

What mesh is best for privacy on outdoor blinds?

A low-openness mesh, around 1–5%, gives good daytime privacy. Lower openness means more privacy but a reduced view out and less airflow.

Do outdoor blinds stop mosquitoes?

They can if the fabric is fine enough and every gap is sealed. Track-guided blinds with sealed sides and a bottom bar that sits on the floor work best.

Are outdoor blinds safe for children?

Cordless blinds are the safest choice around young children. Where a blind has cords, keep them tensioned and secured out of reach.

How do I stop my dog getting under an outdoor blind?

Make sure the bottom bar sits on the floor and use a bottom-bar lock to hold the blind down. A track-guided blind also stops the fabric edges being pushed aside.
