Can you put outdoor blinds on an apartment balcony?

Balconies are some of the most exposed outdoor spaces in a city, and the most regulated. The blind itself is simple; the building is what you plan around.

Balconies · 7 min read · Updated November 2026

Short answer: Usually yes, but in an apartment you will almost always need written approval from the owners corporation, strata or body corporate first, because the blind changes the building's exterior. Choose a track-guided blind that holds its shape in wind, and plan to fix into concrete or the balcony's structural frame.

An outdoor blind can turn a balcony you avoid into one you use — shaded in summer, sheltered from the wind, private from the building opposite. But a balcony is shared property in a way a backyard is not, so the order of operations matters: approval first, then the blind.

Do you need strata approval for balcony blinds?

In most apartment buildings, yes. Balcony blinds are visible from the street and change the look of the façade, so they generally fall under the building's by-laws about alterations to common property or the external appearance of a lot. The exact rules depend on your state's strata legislation and your building's own by-laws, and they vary widely.

What to do:

  1. Read your by-laws for anything about balcony screens, blinds, awnings or external appearance. Many buildings already have an approved specification — a set colour, fabric or style.
  2. Ask the strata manager how applications are made and how long they take. Some need a committee vote; others can be approved by the manager.
  3. Submit details in writing: blind type, fabric and colour, headbox and track colour, fixing method and a photo or sketch of where it will go.
  4. Wait for written approval before you order. A custom blind cannot be returned because the committee wants a different colour.

Renting? You will need your landlord's written consent as well as any strata approval, and agreement on whether the blind stays when you leave. Ask before you drill anything.

Which blind copes with wind on a balcony?

Wind is the defining problem on balconies. It increases with height, it funnels between buildings, and it gusts from directions a ground-level verandah never sees.

On anything above the first couple of floors, a blind with captured edges is the safe choice. An unguided blind that swings in a gust can damage balustrade glass or the blind itself, and in a strong wind any outdoor blind should be raised.

How do you fix blinds to a concrete balcony?

Most apartment balconies are concrete slab above and below, with masonry, rendered or clad walls at the sides.

  • Headbox: fixed to the underside of the slab above, or to the face of a bulkhead, using masonry anchors suited to concrete. A hammer drill and the correct masonry bit are essential.
  • Side tracks: fixed to the side walls or to a post. If the balcony has glass balustrades and no side walls, the tracks need a post — check with strata whether one can be installed.
  • Waterproofing: never drill into the balcony floor or through a waterproofing membrane. Bottom-bar locks that need a floor fixing are usually not suitable on a balcony; choose a system that locks into the side tracks instead.

Before drilling into concrete, check whether the building has post-tensioned slabs. Drilling into a post-tensioned slab can damage the steel tendons inside. Your strata manager or building documentation should say; if it is unclear, have a professional scan the slab first.

Should balcony blinds be manual or motorised?

On a typical balcony opening up to about 3 m wide and within easy reach, a manual track-guided blind is simple and has nothing to wire. Motorisation starts to make sense when the blind is large, the headbox is high under a tall slab, or there are several blinds you want to raise together when the wind picks up.

Motorising in an apartment adds one more approval step. A hard-wired motor needs a licensed electrician and a power source at the headbox, and running new cabling on the outside of the building may itself need strata approval. Battery or rechargeable motors avoid the cabling, which can make approval simpler. Whichever you choose, include it in your strata application so the committee approves the whole installation at once.

Colour and appearance rules

Buildings that allow blinds often specify the colour so the façade stays consistent. Common approaches are a nominated fabric colour (often charcoal or a neutral), a headbox and track colour matched to the window frames, and sometimes a single approved blind type. Following an existing specification is the fastest way through approval — the committee has already said yes to it once.

How should you measure a balcony opening?

Balconies bring their own measuring quirks. The slab above is rarely perfectly level, and the floor often falls towards a drain. Measure the width at the top, middle and bottom, and the drop at both ends and the middle. If the blind sits behind a balustrade, measure from the underside of the slab to the floor, not to the top of the glass, unless you want the blind to stop at balustrade height.

Also check the depth available under the slab. If there is a bulkhead, downlight or sprinkler head in the way, the headbox may need to face fix to a bulkhead or sit further forward. Fire sprinklers in particular must not be obstructed; if one is near the headbox line, ask the strata manager before going further.

Practical points for apartment life

  • Access: everything has to come through the lift and your apartment. Headboxes for wide blinds are long; measure the lift and corridors if the blind is over about 3 m.
  • Noise: drilling concrete is loud. Check the building's permitted hours for works.
  • Drainage: keep the bottom bar clear of the balcony's floor drains and any outlet in the balustrade line.
  • Neighbours: a blind on a corner balcony can affect a neighbour's view. Mentioning it early saves friction later.

Common questions

Do I need body corporate approval for balcony blinds?

In most buildings, yes. Balcony blinds usually change the external appearance of the building, so they need written approval from the strata, owners corporation or body corporate under your by-laws.

What is the best outdoor blind for a windy balcony?

A track-guided blind, where the fabric edge is held in side tracks, is the best choice for most balconies. It holds its shape in wind instead of billowing or swinging against the glass.

Can renters install outdoor blinds on a balcony?

Only with the landlord's written consent, plus strata approval if the building requires it. Agree in writing whether the blind stays or is removed when you move out.

Can you drill into a concrete balcony ceiling?

Usually, with masonry anchors and a hammer drill, but check first whether the slab is post-tensioned. Drilling into post-tensioned concrete can damage the steel tendons, so have it scanned if you are unsure.

What if my balcony has glass balustrades and no side walls?

The side tracks need something solid to fix to, so a post at each side is usually required. Check with your strata whether posts can be installed before ordering.
